# my-first-project
This project focuses on the analysis of insurance data to identify patterns, trends and factors that influence insurance policyholders, their vehicles, customer behavior patterns and their claim. The main objectives of the study are to use data analysis to uncover insight that can support decision making like marketing, services, optimize premiums and reduce claim cost within the industry, the dataset used for the projects contains anonymized information about the policyholders, their vehicles and their claim histories. 
The analysis used a dataset covering 37,542 policyholders with a total claim amount of $1.88 billion, average claim amount of $50,029 per policyholder, zero- claim rate 72.5% no claim filed and average claim frequency of 0.51 claims per policyholder. The analysis entails trends across demographics, vehicle correlation, coverage zones and behavioral factors.
The project highlights the data driven discovery in claim frequencies and amounts and understand the demographics and characteristics of policyholders, developed targeted marketing strategies to attract and retain customers and some recommendation insights that can help the company make informed and strategic decisions.

Aim and Objective of the Analysis

Aim 
•	To help the company improve its services, optimize premiums, and reduce claims cost.

Objectives
•	To examine customer data patterns in claim frequencies and amounts.
•	To understand the demographics and characteristics of policyholders.
•	To develop targeted marketing strategies and attract and retain customers.

Recommendations
The following recommendations are drawn from a comprehensive analysis of 37,541 policyholders across six analytical dimensions: claim frequency and amount, risk assessment, customer segmentation, demographic analysis, geographical analysis, and customer behaviour. Each recommendation is grounded in specific data findings and is designed to drive measurable improvements in profitability, risk management, and customer retention.

1. Convert the Standard Segment Upward
The Standard segment comprising 10,266 policyholders at 27.3% of the portfolio with an average household income of $106,869 sits directly between the Budget-Safe and High-Value tiers. A meaningful subset of this group, estimated at approximately 4,800 policyholders, holds household incomes at or above $125,000 but carries a claim frequency between 1 and 2, placing them just outside the High-Value classification. The company should design a targeted upgrade campaign offering incentivized premium products, safe driver discounts, and enhanced coverage benefits to nudge this near-High-Value audience into the top segment.

Develop a specialised young-driver add-on product. 3-kids driving households cost $52,330 avg — a clear revenue opportunity through tailored coverage.


2. Launch a High-Value Customer Loyalty Programme
With 20,166 policyholders representing 53.7% of the portfolio classified as High-Value, this segment is the company's most profitable and most strategically important. These customers carry an average household income of $187,170, a claim frequency of just 0.18, and are evenly distributed across all coverage zones, making them accessible through broad national campaigns. The company should design and launch a tiered loyalty programme that rewards zero and low-claim behaviour with no-claim bonuses, premium discounts, and exclusive coverage benefits. Special focus should be placed on the 5,089 High-Value customers aged 61–74, who represent long-tenure policyholders at the highest risk of churn and should be prioritized for proactive retention outreach.

3. Apply Zone-Specific Premium Adjustments
The geographical analysis revealed consistent and directional differences in claim behaviour across coverage zones. Suburban and Highly Urban zones generate the highest claim frequencies, while Urban records the highest average claim amount at $50,376. Highly Rural policyholders, on the other hand, show the lowest claim frequency and balanced claim costs — making them the most attractive acquisition target from a risk perspective. The company should introduce zone-adjusted premium loading for Suburban and Highly Urban zones to reflect their higher frequency risk, apply severity-based loading for Urban policyholders, and offer competitively priced entry-level products in Highly Rural zones to grow market share among the safest geographical segment.

4. Reprice the High-Income High-Risk Segment
The High-Income High-Risk segment — 1,810 policyholders at 4.8% of the portfolio — is a uniquely challenging group. They carry the same high average household income of $187,168 as High-Value customers but generate claim frequencies of 3.48, making them among the costliest policyholders in the book. Their financial capacity means they can absorb higher premiums without necessarily churning, presenting an opportunity to reprice this segment's policies upward to properly reflect their risk cost. Targeted risk-based premium reviews for this group, potentially accompanied by offers of behavioural incentive schemes such as telematics or safe driving rewards, could either improve their risk profile or recover appropriate revenue from their elevated claim activity.

5. Develop a Family and Young Driver Upsell Campaign
The customer behaviour analysis identified a clear inflection point at the 3-kids-driving household level, where claim frequency rises to 0.54 and average claim amounts spike to $52,330 the highest in the entire dataset. With 11,000 parents currently holding active policies with kids driving, and 5,798 of these classified as High-Value, this group represents both a significant risk and a significant revenue opportunity. The company should develop a specialized young driver add-on product that provides enhanced coverage for households with multiple young drivers, priced to reflect the elevated risk while remaining attractive to high-income families. Marketing this product directly to the 5,798 High-Value parent segments, who combine financial capacity with demonstrated low overall risk would maximize uptake while managing exposure.
